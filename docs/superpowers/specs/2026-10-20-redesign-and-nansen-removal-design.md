# Bet or Book: site redesign and move from Nansen to the Hyperliquid API

Date: 2026-10-20. Status: approved by the owner in chat on 2026-10-20. Two independent workstreams, built in separate branches.

## Why

1. **Nansen has to go.** The Nansen API terms allow internal business use only and forbid passing their data on externally. Bet or Book does exactly that: public cards, embeds, JSON, PNGs. The owner decided on 13 October to remove Nansen entirely instead of asking for permission. The live site still reads Nansen and still says "Powered by Nansen API".
2. **The site needs one visual system.** The owner commissioned a redesign (handoff folder `design_handoff_bet_or_book`, zipped as `Редизайн сайта о позициях.zip` in the repo root, untracked): four screens, one card pattern, one primary action per screen, clear navigation.

## Sources of truth

- Redesign: the handoff `README.md` (tokens, components, screens, behaviour), the prototype `Bet or Book.dc.html` with `support.js`, and four screenshots. It is high fidelity. The prototype is a reference, not code to copy.
- Migration: `docs/internal/NANSEN_REMOVAL_PLAN_2026-10-14.md` (local only, git-excluded). It has the Nansen-to-Hyperliquid mapping, replacements for what Hyperliquid cannot give, the step list with files and tests, and ten open questions. Read it in full before planning.
- Current behaviour: `README.md` (rules table, honest limits) and `docs/architecture.md`.

## Goals

- **Redesign:** all four screens (Check, Reading, Explore, Saved), header nav, breadcrumbs, position card, "next step" panel and footer as specified in the handoff, running on the real data and logic of the current site.
- **Migration:** a check that reads positions from every Hyperliquid perp dex (HIP-3 included), balances on Ethereum, Arbitrum and HyperEVM from public explorers, funding links from the Hyperliquid ledger plus explorer first-inbound transfers, and 30-day PnL from `portfolio`. No Nansen call anywhere in the code path, no Nansen key, no credits. Superseded 2026-11-10 in two places (see the stage 3 outcome below): balances come from public JSON-RPC nodes, not explorers, and funding links come from the Blockscout explorer's first inbound transaction alone, not from the Hyperliquid ledger as well.
- Verdict rules (`computeVerdict`, 22 reason codes) are unchanged. Only inputs and wording change.

## Non-goals

- No new verdict rules, no new product features, no pricing or paid formats.
- No rewrite or re-attribution of saved readings, the 9 featured entries or the 307 archive entries. They stay dated facts. Four featured examples are re-read through the new pipeline and placed on top; the old ones stay as superseded.
- No mobile app, no new surfaces, no Nansen as an optional layer.

## Anti-goals (a failure even if it technically works)

- Anything shown as if Hyperliquid or explorers had read it while Nansen did, or the reverse. Attribution follows the source of each reading until the migration lands.
- Weaker honesty. Every limit in README "Honest limits" stays: hedges outside the chains read are invisible, a funding link is not ownership, a rule that claims absence needs a complete read of that source. The card must say which chains were read.
- A silently wrong verdict after the move. Every golden snapshot difference is read field by field and must be explained by the change of source.
- Spending Nansen credits or asking the owner for keys before they are needed.

## Constraints

- **No production deploy until the Colosseum results (expected 11-21 November), unless the owner says otherwise.** Judges may open the live site and the submission materials describe the current version. Work happens in branches; verification is local and by tests. If the live site starts failing on its own, tell the owner and propose shipping stage 1 early.
- Local preview must not draw on paid Nansen checks. After the move this is moot; until then verify the UI through saved readings.
- Public repo is English-only. Plain hyphens only, never the long dash, in code, comments, docs and commit messages.
- Cloudflare Worker, KV and Durable Object stack stays. Hyperliquid info limit is 1200 weight per minute per IP and Workers share egress IPs: cache `perpDexs`, honour `retry-after`, one retry.
- Existing share links (`/?s=<id>`, `?address=`, `#saved`, `#explore`, `/embed?s=`) must keep working through the redesign.
- Account creation and key issuing at third-party providers is done by the owner, not by the builder.

## Decisions

Made by the owner: remove Nansen entirely; adopt the redesign; hold deploys until judging ends; do not close the Nansen account until the Colosseum has ended (closing obliges deleting cached copies); release in two steps after the results, the redesign first and the Nansen move second; the two existing Alchemy keys (in the Hermes `.env`, shared with her bots) are the keys to use, the spare one for this site. Superseded 2026-11-09: Alchemy is rejected on its terms and balances come from public nodes with no keys; see the balance-source bullet under Stage 2 outcome below.

- Stage 3 outcome (2026-11-10; its Execution record is in `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-09-nansen-removal-stage-3-pnl-links-and-removal.md`). A check sends no Nansen request. The 30-day PnL comes from Hyperliquid's `portfolio`, the funding links from a block explorer, and the Nansen client, the credit meter's wiring and the live call ledger are removed. A read-only live check of the Abraxas wallet on 2026-11-10 sent 26 requests (19 Hyperliquid, 6 node reads, 1 explorer) and none to Nansen. Seven decisions follow (the seventh, (g), is the keyed shape seen live later the same day).
- (a) The explorer source: Blockscout. Funding links are the first inbound transactions of the address on Ethereum and on Arbitrum from Blockscout (REST API v2 for Ethereum; Arbitrum is read through the Etherscan-style `txlist` since the first live run with a key, see decision (g)). Terms and facts read on 2026-11-09: the public per-chain hosts "sit behind bot protection" and scripted requests "can receive a `403` response whose body is an HTML 'Just a moment...' challenge page"; "per-instance API support has been deprecated" in favour of the universal API at `https://api.blockscout.com`, key as `Authorization: Bearer <key>` (also an `apikey` query parameter, not used here), REST path `https://api.blockscout.com/{chain_id}/api/v2/addresses/{address}/transactions` with `filter=to`, `sort=block_number`, `order=asc`. Free tier: "100K credits/day · 5 RPS · All chains", 20 credits per call (about 5,000 calls a day), 429 over the RPS limit. Keyless `https://eth.blockscout.com/api/v2/addresses/<address>/transactions?filter=to&sort=block_number&order=asc` answered 200 from the dev machine on 2026-11-09 (Abraxas: first inbound transaction from `0xed0c6079229e2d407672a117c22b62064f4a4312`, 2025-07-08, 0.02 ETH; this is the funder the September Nansen fixture also named); `https://arbitrum.blockscout.com` answered 403 with the challenge page. The key is the optional Worker secret `BLOCKSCOUT_API_KEY`, sent only in the `Authorization` header. Without it Ethereum is read keyless and Arbitrum is not read, so the funder search is partial and a short that needs it reads Unknown. An answer that is not a valid list (a challenge page, a refusal, a body that is not JSON) is never an empty answer: it is a chain not read.
- (b) The first-funder rule, and its parity limit. Nansen's "First Funder" is replaced by the sender of the earliest inbound native-coin transaction with value above zero to the address, on Ethereum and on Arbitrum. Token transfers are not used (a spam airdrop would otherwise become the "first funder"); a sender equal to the address is skipped; a failed transaction is skipped, and a pending one makes the page unreadable (the chain reads as not read); only the first page of the explorer's answer (50 transactions, oldest first) is read, and a page with no qualifying transaction but more pages behind it reads as incomplete, not as "no funder". The explorer's labels and tags decide one thing, whether the sender is a shared service (an exchange or bridge, skipped) or unverified; a label that matches no service pattern vouches for nothing and stays unverified, and the label text is never stored or shown. The parity limit: funding that arrives through internal transactions (contract or batch withdrawals, Arbitrum L1-to-L2 deposits) is not in the `/transactions` list and is not seen, so "first funder" means the first direct sender and may differ from Nansen's. With real Blockscout tags a funder Nansen left unlabelled may read as an exchange and be skipped (Blockscout tags the Abraxas Arbitrum funder `0xb38e8c17e38363af6ebdcb3dae12e0243582891d` as a Binance hot wallet on Ethereum), which would change the September flagship figures, so the four featured examples are re-read with a key and compared field by field. Seen in the keyed capture of 2026-11-10 (decision (g)): the tag is on the Ethereum chain's transaction lists, so the Abraxas Arbitrum funder is skipped as an exchange and a new reading follows only the Ethereum funder. The README says all of this.
- (c) Hyperliquid ledger links are not used. The goals of 2026-10-20 named the Hyperliquid ledger as a second source of funding links. Nansen's related-wallets answer, which the explorer replaces, was on-chain data on two chains, so the explorer's first inbound transfer is the parity replacement; transfers between Hyperliquid accounts were never in the reading, and adding them would change what "First Funder" means on the card. If they are ever wanted, that is a new rule with its own review.
- (d) The credit budget is removed and the Durable Object class is retired. A check makes no paid call, so there is nothing to meter: the daily cap, the account floor, the worst-case reservation, the reservation and settlement in the `/api/check` handler, the staged Nansen wrapper and the live call recorder are gone, with the `NANSEN_DAILY_CREDIT_CAP` and `NANSEN_CREDIT_FLOOR` variables. The per-check request budget of 45 stays, and the check's own rate limits. The `NansenBudget` class and its `NANSEN_BUDGET` binding stay under their names (deleting a Durable Object class takes a migration) as a retired class that answers 410. `/api/ledger` serves the static September archive with `live: null`; the Worker's own September calls, which the retired meter counted, are added to the archive at release (read production's `/api/ledger` once, before the deploy). Telemetry writes no cost fields; lines written before keep theirs.
- (e) What the missing PRO terms mean for the release. No terms page for the Blockscout PRO API on display, storage or attribution could be found on 2026-11-09 (https://dev.blockscout.com is a script-only page, its `/terms` is a 404). The site displays Blockscout-derived facts (a first funder's address and date) on public cards, embeds, JSON and pictures and keeps dated readings, so by the source-terms rule above the explorer is adopted conditionally. The owner registers at https://dev.blockscout.com (free, no card), reads the terms shown at sign-up and says whether they limit displaying, storing or redistributing data or require attribution. Until a key exists, Arbitrum funding links are not read, every funder search is partial, and a short that needs one reads Unknown instead of Bet: honest and safe, but "Looks like a bet" becomes unreachable for most shorts. If the terms forbid the use, the explorer is replaced or the funding links are dropped, and the bet rule is not loosened to compensate. The key is deployed as a Worker secret that the owner types; the builder never sees it. The keyless public Ethereum host is not covered by any terms found either and may start refusing at any time.
- (f) The attribution rule, in one sentence: a reading's credit names exactly the sources it used, `Powered by Nansen API` (post: `Powered by @nansen_ai`) unchanged for a reading made with Nansen and otherwise `Data: Hyperliquid API` plus `public nodes` when balances were read from them and `Blockscout` when funding links were, on the publisher DTO and the embed, the share text, the downloaded card and the link-preview picture (`attributionOf` in `src/engine/attribution.ts`; one rule, `madeWithExplorer`, tells a new reading from an old one, and the standing picture for a link with no picture yet credits no source).
- (g) The keyed shape, seen live on 2026-11-10 (read-only, with the owner's free key; 20 credits a call, 100K credits a day, 5 requests a second). The key was read from the git-ignored `.dev.vars`, sent only in the `Authorization` header and printed nowhere. (1) Ethereum on the REST path, `https://api.blockscout.com/1/api/v2/addresses/<address>/transactions?filter=to&sort=block_number&order=asc`, answers 200 in the same v2 shape as the keyless host (`items`, `next_page_params`); the reader finds `0xed0c...4312` for Abraxas. The response headers carry `x-credits-remaining` and `x-ratelimit-remaining`. Ethereum is unchanged. (2) Arbitrum on the same REST path with `sort=block_number&order=asc` answers HTTP 500 `{"error":"Internal server error","source":"upstream"}`: Blockscout's Arbitrum instance does not support that sort, and without it the list is newest first, which cannot give the earliest funder. The Arbitrum REST address-info endpoint also answered 500. (3) Arbitrum therefore uses the Etherscan-style form, `https://api.blockscout.com/v2/api?chain_id=42161&module=account&action=txlist&address=<address>&sort=asc&page=1&offset=50`, key in the header: HTTP 200 `{"status":"1","message":"OK","result":[...]}` with rows of strings (`blockNumber`, `timeStamp`, `from`, `to`, `value`, `isError`, `txreceipt_status` and more), both directions in one list, oldest first, addresses in lowercase. For Abraxas the first row is from `0xb38e8c17e38363af6ebdcb3dae12e0243582891d`, 0.2 ETH, `timeStamp` 1740071909 (2025-02-20T17:18:29Z): the same funder and the same moment the September Nansen read named (`test/fixtures/nansen/related-wallets-abraxas-arbitrum.json`), which replaces the hand-written Arbitrum page. `filterby=to`, and a `startblock`/`endblock` range, made it answer `"status":"0","message":"No transactions found"` for an address that has transactions, so neither is sent and the direction is filtered in code; an address with no transactions at all answers `"status":"0","message":"No transactions found","result":[]`, the one answer read as "nothing came in". Every other envelope, field or order rejects the chain, as the REST page does. (4) Labels. The txlist carries none, and the Ethereum address-info endpoint answers `metadata: null` and `name: null` for `0xb38e...891d`, although the Ethereum transaction lists tag it ("Binance: Hot Wallet", "Exchange", "Binance 54"): Blockscout attaches the tags to the parties of a transaction list, not to the address answer. So the label of an Arbitrum sender comes from one extra request, the sender's own Ethereum transaction list, reading the tags of the party that is the sender, and it is used only to skip an exchange (an exchange funds from the same address on both chains; a contract wallet labelled on Ethereum and unrelated on Arbitrum is the rare case this gets wrong). The lookup is best effort and never an error of the chain read: a refusal, a timeout, a wrong shape or a request the budget refuses leaves the sender unverified, which fails safe - an unlabelled exchange funder is followed, and its matching holdings make the reading Unknown, never a Bet. (5) Cost: a check with an Arbitrum link spends 3 explorer requests (60 credits), so the heaviest tested check is 42 requests (30 Hyperliquid, 3 explorer, 9 node reads) and the reserve a short keeps for the funder search is 9 (`LINKS_RESERVE`); `CHECK_REQUEST_LIMIT` stays 45. Still open: the explorer has been called from a development machine only, never from Cloudflare's network.
